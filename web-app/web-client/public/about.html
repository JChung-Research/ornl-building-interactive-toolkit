Why it matters

Buildings consume approximately 73% of the electricity produced in the U.S., presenting a significant opportunity for energy optimization. Through advanced automation and controls that respond to grid needs without compromising occupant comfort, building energy consumption can be reduced by 20-30%. This mutual benefit empowers building occupants with greater control over energy use while reducing power generation costs and supporting grid resilience.

Platform Overview

Our web-based building energy management platform addresses the critical gap in small and medium-sized commercial buildings, which comprise 94% of U.S. commercial buildings yet have lacked comprehensive automation infrastructure. The platform leverages open-source VOLTTRON middleware and industry-standard communication protocols (BACnet and Modbus) to integrate diverse building systems through an streamlined, containerized system architecture that eliminates vendor lock-in, complex manual configuration, and specialized technical expertise requirements.

What the platform enables

The platform currently manages diverse building portfolios including ORNL's Building 3147, industry partner facilities, with plans to integrate PNNL buildings. Through its use-friendly web interface, building operators can monitor real-time energy consumption, environmental conditions, and HVAC performance across heterogeneous device ecosystems while implementing automated control strategies that optimize energy efficiency and occupant compfort.

Research Roadmap

This work supports ORNL's broader buildings-to-grid research initiative, which combines low-cost wireless sensor technology with advanced control strategies. Future enhancements will integrate integrate transactive control capabilities, enabling grid-responsive load management and maximizing utilization of distributed energy resources like photovoltaic systems and battery storage. Through partnership with Southern Company and other utilities, these innovations will deliver cost savings to building owners while enhancing community-level power resilience and grid reliability.
